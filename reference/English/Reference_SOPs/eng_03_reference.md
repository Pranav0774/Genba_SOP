## Steps

1. Plan the stand and make a cut list.
2. Cut all parts for the main frame from 2x4 pine.
3. Support long pieces with a scrap block for a stable cut.
4. Mark the screw locations on a scrap piece and transfer the marks to every matching part.
5. Drill pilot holes from the outside of the joint.
6. Counterbore about 1 inch deep with a larger bit so the 4-inch screws sink in.
7. Apply glue to each joint.
8. Clamp the pieces together before driving screws.
9. Drive the 4-inch screws to assemble the first side sub-assembly.
10. Assemble the second side sub-assembly the same way.
11. Join the two sides with the rails: offset the new screw holes from the earlier screws, clamp, and do one side at a time.
12. Add supplemental blocks under the legs so the caster screws are not in end grain.
13. Attach a 3-inch locking caster to each leg with 4 screws.
14. Cut 1x4 boards into slats for the bottom shelf and the top.
15. Mark notches for the legs on the two outer slats.
16. Cut the notches with a jigsaw.
17. Attach each slat with a single screw in the middle so the wood can expand and contract, spacing the slats by eye.
18. On the top, leave a small overhang on each side and space the slats evenly.
19. Place the planer on the stand.

## Tips (not scored as steps)

- Use pocket screws if you have a pocket-hole jig.
- Glue is near its working-temperature limit in a cold garage.

## Tools and materials

- Saw for cutting 2x4s [check: which saw is shown]
- Drill / driver
- Drill bits (pilot bit and larger counterbore bit)
- 4-inch screws
- Wood glue
- Clamps
- Jigsaw
- 3-inch locking casters (4)
- 2x4 pine boards
- 1x4 boards
- Scrap wood
- Pencil
- Planning software (SketchUp)

## Safety points

- Support long pieces (scrap block) so the cut is stable.
- Clamp pieces before driving screws so they cannot slip.
- Use locking casters so the stand does not move while stock is fed through the planer.
